Melody roll

  • Time runs left to right, as in the guitar Notes view and the waveform. Pitch goes up. The now line sits a fifth of the way in, with about two bars of look-ahead.
  • The pitch rail shows the key: in-key rows are paper, out-of-key rows are sunken, and the tonic (A♭) rows are ruled. Labels show note names or scale degrees, and you can turn them off once you know the song.
  • “Follow phrase” fits the rows to the notes on screen (at least an octave), easing over 400 ms, so labels stay at reading size. “Whole song” keeps one fixed map for spatial memory. On a phone it is about 11 px per row.
  • Confidence matches the guitar view: medium is lighter ink, low is a dashed outline with “?”. Octave slips are left as transcribed, and stay low-confidence.

Phrases without lyrics

  • A phrase is a run of notes split at rests of at least ¾ beat (0.6 s minimum). Runs longer than 4 bars split at their largest gap. There's no new ML stage: it's a pure function of notes.vocals and the beat grid, so it stays re-runnable when notes are corrected.
  • Markers along the top: the phrase number and its bar span, and a breath mark (’) where each phrase ends. Tap one to loop it. Phrase loops snap to beats with one beat of pre-roll, because sung phrases rarely start on a downbeat.
  • Repeated phrases (same interval shape, within 1 semitone) share a letter, A A′ B…, which works like lyrics for finding the chorus again. In the mockup this is approximate.
  • The strip shows this phrase, its first note (with a “First note” cue so you can pitch-match before you come in), the next phrase and the countdown to it, and your vocal range.

Singer controls

  • Guide vocal On / Quiet / Off is the vocal stem's level in the mixer, brought forward because it's the main thing a singer changes. The rest of the mixer stays where it is.
  • Everything is keyboard and pedal operable: previous/next phrase, loop the phrase, first-note cue.
  • Range is the 5th–95th percentile of confident notes. That makes “Transpose to fit my range” cheap once pitch shift lands (V-feature, ±3 semitone warning on vocal stems).

Later, on the same canvas
