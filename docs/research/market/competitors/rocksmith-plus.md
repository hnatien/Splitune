# Competitor teardown: Rocksmith+ (Ubisoft)

## TL;DR
- Rocksmith+ is Ubisoft's subscription successor to Rocksmith 2014: learn guitar, bass and piano by playing real songs with real-time feedback and adaptive difficulty; PC, PlayStation, iOS, Android under one account.
- Free tier: lessons plus 20 songs; paid unlocks the licensed catalog ("thousands" of songs). Launch pricing (June 2021 announcement): $14.99/mo, $39.99/3 mo, $99.99/12 mo; Ubisoft now advertises discounts as low as $6/mo on a 12-month plan.
- Troubled reception: Steam shows "Overwhelmingly Negative" (18% of 1,421 English reviews positive) as of fetch. The team is now smaller; licensing had to be renegotiated for the whole catalog, so Rocksmith 2014 DLC did not carry over.
- Rocksmith+ has a user-generated content system (Rocksmith Workshop/UGC) to add licensed songs with transcription tools.
- Splitune takeaway: a licensed-catalog subscription is expensive to build and politically fragile; users nonetheless want "play real songs with feedback".

## Findings

### Core features
Real-time feedback, difficulty adjusting as you play, expert video lessons, Riff Repeater, Practice Presets, Skills Profiles, one account across PC/PlayStation/mobile; guitar, bass and piano under one subscription ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2834910/Rocksmith/), [Ubisoft](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/game/rocksmith)). At launch: visual interface, improved note detection, tablature option, MIDI playback, playlists, a Connect app letting a phone's mic track an acoustic guitar, and Rocksmith Workshop for creating arrangements of licensed songs ([Ubisoft launch post, Jun 2021](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/game/rocksmith/plus/news-updates/5fj6ukWblN7LVkQc5CZRlg/rocksmith-offers-more-guitar-music-learning-than-ever)).

### UX flow of the "learn a song" path
Select a licensed song → play along in a game-like highway with live note detection through a guitar cable (or mic via Connect) → adaptive difficulty ramps arrangement up → Riff Repeater to drill sections. Users can only play songs in the catalog (plus UGC arrangements of licensed songs).

### Instruments and levels
Guitar, bass, piano; beginner to advanced via adaptive difficulty ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2834910/Rocksmith/)).

### Pricing and business model (accessed 2026-10-20)
- Steam: free start with lessons and 20 songs; subscription unlocks the full catalog; no price on the page ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2834910/Rocksmith/)).
- June 2021 launch pricing: $14.99/month, $39.99 for 3 months, $99.99 for 12 months ([Ubisoft launch post](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/game/rocksmith/plus/news-updates/5fj6ukWblN7LVkQc5CZRlg/rocksmith-offers-more-guitar-music-learning-than-ever)).
- July 2026 dev update: discounts bring the 12-month plan to as low as $6 (EUR 6) per month depending on region; Rocksmith 2014 Learn & Play remains a one-time purchase on Steam ([dev update](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-gb/game/rocksmith/plus/news-updates/6QLRU2tVz1xa2X2qFNS9b/rocksmith-dev-team-update-where-we-are-whats-next-and-where-we-stand)).
- The current official price page (ubisoft.com subscription) rendered no prices to our fetcher. Periodic free-play promotions exist (for example 12 tracks in early September 2026) ([Ubisoft](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/game/rocksmith)).

### Platforms (official)
PC (Windows 10/11, 8 GB RAM, 20 GB), PlayStation, iOS, Android ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2834910/Rocksmith/), [Ubisoft](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/game/rocksmith)).

### What is public about tech and company
Ubisoft Massive's Snowdrop engine; note detection claimed "on par with professional strobe tuners" with tuned strictness for vibrato, sustains, chords and bass; AI algorithms for chord chart creation; all music was renegotiated and most Rocksmith 2014 songs did not return at launch; closed beta added 373 songs ([dev diary, Jul 2021](https://www.ubisoft.com/pl-pl/game/rocksmith/plus/news-updates/1R73uTJE3SninROom780EG/rocksmith-dev-diary-july-2021)). July 2026 update: smaller team, focus on stability, new licensed songs (e.g., Metallica, Dream Theater, Coldplay, Linkin Park), UGC to add licensed songs, relicensing removed DLC, and "enforcement actions" against unauthorized use ([dev update](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-gb/game/rocksmith/plus/news-updates/6QLRU2tVz1xa2X2qFNS9b/rocksmith-dev-team-update-where-we-are-whats-next-and-where-we-stand)).

### Standout strengths
Game-like engagement with the actual instrument; adaptive difficulty; one subscription across three instruments; real licensed recordings; UGC arrangements.

### Common complaints (tier 4, anecdotal)
Steam: "Overwhelmingly Negative" overall (18% positive of 1,421) and "Mostly Negative" in the last 30 days (20% of 24), as fetched ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2834910/Rocksmith/)). Specific complaint themes were not extracted because individual reviews were not readable on the page.

### What Splitune can learn or do differently
- Licensing the whole catalog caused lost DLC and a library backlash; a user-chosen-content model avoids that dependency but trades legal risk (`research/legal/youtube-copyright-and-platform-policy.md`).
- Adaptive difficulty from an actual performance is the strongest value proposition; Splitune can derive it from the user's chosen song.
- Cable-based accurate detection vs mic convenience: offer both.

## Implications for Splitune
Rocksmith+ signals that "play real songs with real feedback" is the dream product, and that licensed-catalog execution risk is large. A UGC/transcription pipeline is an alternative.
