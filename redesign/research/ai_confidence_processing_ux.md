# UX patterns for uncertain AI output, human-in-the-loop correction and long-running processing (input for the Splitune redesign)

Research date: 2026-11-08. Scope: (a) showing per-note / per-chord confidence without colour-only encoding and without eroding trust, (b) fast correction of AI output, (c) making a multi-minute, multi-stage pipeline feel fast, transparent and recoverable, plus trust calibration and accessibility. Splitune's current design decisions referenced below come from the repo (`docs/specs/21-v1-features.md`, `26-design-system.md`, `27-screens-flows-and-content.md`), not from web sources.

Source-age flags: PAIR Guidebook (2019, revised 2021) and Microsoft HAX guidelines (CHI 2019) are older but remain the canonical industry guidance; the Logic Pro help pages found are for 10.4 (2018) and the Melodyne Sound On Sound review is from the DNA era (pre-Melodyne 5), so exact UI details may have changed; the WebKit Web Push post is from 2023 (iOS/iPadOS 16.4). Several tool-fetch results came back as summaries rather than raw page text, so short quotes below are as reported by the fetch tool.

## 1. How should per-item confidence be shown (encodings, binned vs continuous, hide vs flag)?

### Takeaway
Industry guidance converges on: only show confidence if it is calibrated and changes what the user does; prefer a few semantic categories (or ranked alternatives) over numbers; and choose *what* to flag by "likely to need fixing", not raw model probability (raw-probability highlighting gave no measurable benefit in a controlled study). Frequency framing ("1 in 10") is more intuitive than percentages. No source found evaluates opacity vs dashed outline vs glyph specifically for notation.

### Cited Findings
- PAIR: "Users shouldn't implicitly trust your AI system in all circumstances"; the goal is trust calibrated to where the system is reliable, with users checking output where it isn't. — [Google PAIR, Explainability + Trust](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/)
- PAIR: decide whether to show confidence at all; skip it if it "isn't impactful" on decisions (fine-grained values can confuse without a clear action) and reconsider it if it "could create mistrust" (e.g. misleadingly high scores leading to blind acceptance); test with diverse users. — [PAIR](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/)
- PAIR's four confidence-display types: **categorical** (High/Medium/Low; "Best match, good match, match unsure") — simple, but cut-offs need care and each category should state the action; **N-best alternatives** ("This photo might be New York, Tokyo, or Los Angeles") — useful when confidence is low, prompts user judgment, number of options needs testing; **numeric** ("93% run match") — risky, assumes users "have a good baseline understanding of probability", and since 100% is rarely reached users may doubt near-certain results; **data visualisations** (error bars, shaded ranges) — often best understood by domain experts. — [PAIR](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/)
- PAIR suggested test probes: "On this scale, show me how trusting you are of this recommendation" and "What, if anything, would increase your trust in this recommendation?" — [PAIR](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/)
- Apple HIG (Machine learning): "If you're not sure how your confidence values correlate with the quality of your results, it's not a good idea to convey confidence to people"; "translate confidence values into concepts that people already understand"; "If you must display confidence directly, consider expressing it in terms of semantic categories"; for proactive features set "a confidence threshold below which you don't offer results"; "When you know that confidence values correspond to result quality, you generally want to avoid showing results when confidence is low." — [Apple HIG, Machine learning](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- Apple HIG on multiple options: "Prefer diverse options", "avoid providing too many options", "List the most likely option first", "Make options easy to distinguish and choose". — [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- Vasconcelos, Bansal, Fourney, Liao, Wortman Vaughan, "Generation Probabilities Are Not Enough: Uncertainty Highlighting in AI Code Completions" (preregistered study, 30 programmers; published in ACM TOCHI per search results): highlighting tokens with the lowest generation probability gave no benefit over no highlighting on any measure; highlighting tokens predicted *likely to be edited* (a separate "edit model" trained on user edits) led to more edits of highlighted tokens and higher perceived utility. Participants wanted explanations of highlights and control over the threshold; preferred highlights that are "granular, informative, interpretable, and not overwhelming". — [arXiv 2302.07248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07248v3)
- Zhang, Liao & Bellamy (ACM FAT* 2020): showing per-case confidence scores helped people calibrate trust (more reliance on high-confidence predictions), but calibration alone did not improve joint decision accuracy; local per-prediction explanations were problematic for calibrating trust. — [arXiv 2001.02114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02114v1)
- Padilla, Kay & Hullman's uncertainty-visualisation handbook chapter (summary table reproduced in course slides) lists **frequency framing** as a theory: uncertainty "is more intuitively understood in a frequency framing (1 out of 10) than in a probabilistic framing (10%)", with hypothetical outcome plots (HOPs) as an example technique; it also lists attribute substitution (viewers swap hard uncertainty judgments for easier ones). — [NIU CS628 lecture slides citing the chapter](https://faculty.cs.niu.edu/~dakoop/cs628-2021fa/lectures/lecture15.pdf)
- HOPs (animated draws of possible outcomes): users made comparable or better judgments of probabilities than with error bars and violin plots in Hullman et al.'s 2015 experiments. — [UW Interactive Data Lab, HOPs](https://medium.com/@uwdata/hypothetical-outcome-plots-hops-help-users-separate-signal-from-noise-870d4e2b75d7)
- Hullman, Qiao, Kale, Kay, Correll (InfoVis 2018) surveyed 86 uncertainty-vis user studies and found evaluation practice leans on performance and satisfaction measures — i.e., the evidence base for specific encodings is uneven. — [In Pursuit of Error](https://www.tableau.com/research/publications/pursuit-error-survey-uncertainty-visualization-evaluation)
- A 2024 review (Mason) notes Padilla et al. (2022) summarised recurring cognitive effects in uncertainty-vis experiments but as separate considerations rather than a unified theory, and that readers misinterpret common uncertainty displays. — [The Noisy Work of Uncertainty Visualisation Research, arXiv 2411.10482](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.10482)
- Jakob Nielsen (personal site, not NN/g) argues AI's probabilistic nature affects all of UX and recommends embracing uncertainty while mitigating its downsides. — [UX Tigers, "Embrace AI's Uncertainty in UX" (2024)](https://www.uxtigers.com/post/ai-uncertainty-ux)
- Splitune today (repo): three ordinal confidence levels encoded by ink value + dashed outline + "?" glyph, identical across notation, chord chips, timeline and badges, all ≥4.5:1 contrast; this replaced an earlier opacity rule that dropped low-confidence digits below 3:1. — `docs/specs/26-design-system.md` §"Confidence is never hue"
- Splitune today (repo): a song whose mean calibrated note confidence is below 0.80 opens on the Chart with a low-confidence banner and "Show notes anyway". — `docs/specs/21-v1-features.md`

### Inferences
- Splitune's 3-level ordinal scheme matches PAIR "categorical" and Apple "semantic categories" guidance; numeric percentages should stay out of the practice UI.
- Opacity-only encoding fails twice: contrast (Splitune already found <3:1) and colour/lightness-only perception. Redundant encoding (value + dash + glyph) is the defensible choice; the glyph is also the one cue that survives monochrome printing/export.
- The Vasconcelos result is the most directly transferable evidence: flagging *every* low-probability note is likely noise. What should get a "?" is the subset a learner would actually need to fix and that matters musically (wrong pitch/fret, octave slip, wrong chord quality/root), ideally estimated from Splitune's own correction overlays or eval data rather than the raw model posterior.
- Song-level threshold (the 0.80 gate) is an application of Apple's "threshold below which you don't offer results", softened into "offer, but flagged and behind a choice" — appropriate because transcription is user-requested and musicians can verify by ear.
- Frequency framing suggests the banner copy should read like "about 1 in 4 notes may be off in this song" rather than "confidence 0.74".
- Calibration is a prerequisite: if a stage (e.g. chords vs notes vs beats) has uncalibrated scores, Apple's guidance says do not surface confidence for that stage at all.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found comparing dashed outlines, hatching, glyph badges and opacity for per-symbol confidence in music notation or tab specifically.
- Could not retrieve the full Padilla/Kay/Hullman chapter text; publication details (handbook, 2022) are from secondary citations. Kay's quantile dotplot work was not fetched.
- No NN/g article specific to AI confidence display was found (searches returned only secondary blogs); a claimed NN/g statistic in one blog could not be verified and was excluded.
- No evidence found on the right density cap (how many flags per bar/page before users ignore them).

## 2. How do existing music-AI products show uncertainty or alternatives, and handle wrong chords?

### Takeaway
None of the surveyed consumer music-AI products was found to show explicit per-item confidence. They handle errors through editing (Klangio, Chordify, Songscription), simplification levels (Moises), community correction (Chordify), re-running with corrected inputs (Klangio Retranscribe), or — in pro tools — a dedicated analysis-correction mode that shows rejected candidates as hollow outlines (Melodyne) or accepts user "hints" before re-analysis (Logic Smart Tempo).

### Cited Findings
- **Melodyne 5 – Note Assignment Mode:** a separate mode for checking detection against what was played; "none of the changes you make has any audible effect on the notes themselves". Solid blobs = active notes (best guess); **hollow silhouettes** = pitches considered but not chosen, visible only in Note Assignment Mode (Melodic/Polyphonic algorithms); users turn a silhouette into a blob (activate) or the reverse. Changing the algorithm triggers fresh analysis that discards those edits. — [Celemony Help Center, Note Assignment Mode](https://helpcenter.celemony.com/M5/doc/melodyneEditor5/en/M5tour_NA_Mode_1?env=live)
- Melodyne (older SOS review): a Note Assignment slider controls how many predicted detection points are displayed vs actively used; high/low pitch limits remove out-of-range blobs en masse; a monitoring synth plays detected pitches back to verify; undo history was lost on entering Note Assignment mode (flag: may be outdated). — [Sound On Sound, Melodyne DNA Editor review](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/celemony-melodyne-dna-editor)
- **Logic Pro Smart Tempo Editor:** "Set Downbeat" handle on a beat marker; Hint mode lets users click to add downbeat hints (upper third) or beat hints (lower third) that help re-detect bars; edits change only tempo information, not the audio. The iPad version offers "shift downbeat" when beat 4 is detected as beat 1. — [Apple Logic Pro help (10.4)](https://help.apple.com/logicpro/mac/10.4/en.lproj/lgcp9281e70c.html); [Logic Pro for iPad guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/logicpro-ipad/lpip99743e51/3.0/ipados/26)
- **Klangio (Guitar2Tabs, Transcription Studio):** Edit Mode button in the transcription viewer; select notes by clicking tab, add notes by typing a fret number, change capo/tuning, transposition, time signature, ties/slurs; requires a paid Full Transcription; help centre says web only. — [Klangio Help Center](https://klang.io/?p=17354)
- Klangio 2025 update: edit, add or delete detected chords in a transcription. — [Klangio Updates](https://klang.io/?p=9373)
- **Klangio Retranscribe** (Jan 2023): users edit "the information that was originally given to the AI during the initial transcription" (named: chords, rhythm) and the app re-runs to produce updated tabs — designed for errors like out-of-sync triplets. — [Klangio blog, Retranscribe](https://klang.io/?p=10950)
- **Moises chord detection:** chords and diagrams sync to playback; easy/medium/advanced chord views (web/desktop), simplified view, capo and notation options (mobile); free accounts get the first minute only; "Advanced Chords" for jazz extensions; transpose via Pitch Changer. No manual chord-edit feature was found in sources. — [Moises Help, How do I use Chord Detection?](https://help.moises.ai/hc/articles/6569274648220-How-do-I-use-Chord-Detection); [Moises chord finder](https://moises.ai/features/chord-finder)
- **Chordify:** detection "doesn't always get them right, so the community is allowed to edit the chords until, eventually, they reach a consensus" (third-party description; may not reflect current UI). Users report tapping a chord to change it and its voicing. — [Hooktheory blog](https://www.hooktheory.com/blog/chordify-alternatives/); [Trustpilot reviews](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/chordify.net?page=4); [MusicMark](https://www.musicmark.org.uk/resources/chordify-chords-for-any-song/)
- **Songscription** (launched mid-2025): browser editor and exports (MIDI, MusicXML, Guitar Pro); the vendor itself says the recording environment affects accuracy more than any other variable; independent reviews treat output as a first pass needing human review. — [Songscription blog](https://www.songscription.ai/blog/accurate-ai-music-transcriptions); [TechCrunch 2025-06-30](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/30/songscription-launches-an-ai-powered-shazam-for-sheet-music); [Ethan Hein on MusicRadar review (covers a different app)](https://www.ethanhein.com/wp/2025/ai-transcription-on-musicradar/)

### Inferences
- Explicit per-item confidence in a consumer practice app would be a differentiator, but there is no market precedent to copy; Splitune must validate it with users (PAIR test probes) rather than assume it builds trust.
- Melodyne's split between "fix the analysis" (no audible change, alternatives visible as silhouettes) and "edit the music" is the closest model for Splitune's overlays: keep practice view clean (only "?" marks), and show candidate alternatives only in an explicit "Fix" mode.
- Logic's hint model (user gives sparse anchors, the system recomputes the rest) and Klangio's Retranscribe (correct upstream inputs, re-run downstream) are two proven ways to make corrections cascade instead of forcing note-by-note editing.
- Melodyne's "changing the algorithm discards your edits" is an anti-pattern to avoid: Splitune re-analysis must keep overlays.
- Moises' easy/medium/advanced levels show that "simplify" is an accepted escape hatch for uncertain chord extensions (e.g. fall back to triads when 7th/9th quality is unsure).

### Gaps
- No primary sources retrieved for AnthemScore, RipX, Ableton audio-to-MIDI/warp markers, Capo, Logic Chord Track/Session Players or Transcribe! beat markers; whether any of them expose confidence is unverified.
- No official Chordify help article on chord editing was found; whether editing is Premium-only and whether alternatives are suggested is unconfirmed.
- Songscription editor features beyond "AI-assisted editor" are undocumented in sources found.

## 3. What correction (human-in-the-loop) patterns work, and how should corrections feed recomputation?

### Takeaway
Guidelines agree: make correction cheap, guided (pick from options) rather than freeform, immediately valuable, and explicit about consequences; never rely on user corrections to paper over bad output. Pro audio tools show corrections as non-destructive analysis edits plus re-detection from user hints.

### Cited Findings
- Amershi et al., "Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction" (CHI 2019), 18 guidelines in four phases. **Initially:** G1 Make clear what the system can do; G2 Make clear how well the system can do what it can do ("how often the AI system may make mistakes"). **During interaction:** G3 Time services based on context; G4 Show contextually relevant information; G5 Match relevant social norms; G6 Mitigate social biases. **When wrong:** G7 Support efficient invocation; G8 Support efficient dismissal; G9 Support efficient correction ("Make it easy to edit, refine, or recover when the AI system is wrong"); G10 Scope services when in doubt ("disambiguation or gracefully degrade"); G11 Make clear why the system did what it did. **Over time:** G12 Remember recent interactions; G13 Learn from user behavior; G14 Update and adapt cautiously ("Limit disruptive changes"); G15 Encourage granular feedback; G16 Convey the consequences of user actions; G17 Provide global controls; G18 Notify users about changes. — [Amershi et al. 2019 (PDF)](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2019/01/Guidelines-for-Human-AI-Interaction-camera-ready.pdf)
- In the validation study (49 practitioners, 20 products, 785 examples: 313 applications, 277 violations), G2, G11 and G17 were relevant but not widely implemented; G11 had among the most violations. The authors say the guidelines are not a simple checklist. — [Amershi et al. 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2019/01/Guidelines-for-Human-AI-Interaction-camera-ready.pdf); [Microsoft Research project](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/)
- Apple HIG, corrections: "Give people familiar, easy ways to make corrections"; "Provide immediate value when people make a correction"; "Never rely on corrections to make up for low-quality results"; "When possible, use guided corrections instead of freeform corrections". — [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- Apple HIG, mistakes: "Anticipate mistakes"; "Make it easy for people to correct frequent or predictable mistakes"; limitations: "Suggest alternative ways to accomplish the goal instead of showing no results". — [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- Apple HIG, explicit feedback: "Request explicit feedback only when necessary"; "Always make providing explicit feedback a voluntary task"; "Act immediately when you receive explicit feedback and persist the resulting changes". — [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- PAIR: give users control through preferences, corrections and feedback; "Automate more when trust is high, or risk of error is low"; to regain trust, explain the recovery plan ahead of time and in the moment, and let users switch to manual control for high-risk outcomes. — [PAIR](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/)
- Logic Smart Tempo: downbeat fix by "Set Downbeat" handle or by adding beat/downbeat hints that drive re-detection; edits are tempo metadata only. — [Apple Logic Pro help](https://help.apple.com/logicpro/mac/10.4/en.lproj/lgcp9281e70c.html)
- Melodyne: corrections to detection are non-audible analysis edits; silhouettes (unchosen candidates) can be activated with a click. — [Celemony Help Center](https://helpcenter.celemony.com/M5/doc/melodyneEditor5/en/M5tour_NA_Mode_1?env=live)
- Klangio Retranscribe: correct the inputs given to the AI (chords, rhythm), then re-run to get new tabs. — [Klangio](https://klang.io/?p=10950)
- Vasconcelos et al.: users asked for control over highlight thresholds and explanations of why something is highlighted. — [arXiv 2302.07248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07248v3)

### Inferences
- Chord fixes should be *guided*: tapping an unsure chord offers 2–3 model alternatives (most likely first, diverse), each auditionable over the loop, with "other…" as the freeform fallback (Apple guided corrections + N-best).
- Note/tab fixes map to predictable-mistake shortcuts: octave ±12, same pitch on another string/fret, delete ghost note, merge/split note — each one key/pedal action, plus "jump to next unsure item".
- Beat/downbeat fixes should cascade: "This is beat 1" / "shift bars by one beat" / tap hints, then re-quantize chords and tab in the browser with `music-core` (instant, "immediate value"), showing the consequence before commit (G16: "Re-barring 84 bars — Undo").
- Provenance matters for trust and for overlays: edited items need a visible, non-colour "edited by you" mark, per-item "revert to transcription", a global "show original" toggle and undo/redo history (G9, G17).
- G13 ("learn from user behavior") should be applied carefully: in personal mode corrections are saved per song, not used for retraining, so copy must not imply the model learns (G2 honesty).
- Bulk fixes (apply a chord correction to every identical occurrence in repeated sections) are a reasonable extension of G9 but no source tested them; treat as a hypothesis to test.

### Gaps
- No empirical study found on correction speed for music transcription UIs (e.g. pick-from-alternatives vs direct entry).
- Ableton warp-marker UX and Transcribe! beat-marker UX were not sourced.
- No source found on how to present conflicts when a re-analysis disagrees with existing user overlays.

## 4. How should a multi-minute, multi-stage pipeline be presented (progress, ETA, leave-and-return, notifications, partial results, failure, cancel)?

### Takeaway
For waits over ~10 seconds, use a determinate (percent-done) indicator with a rough, padded time estimate and a stated reason; let users stop; show real work being done (operational transparency raises perceived value) but avoid exposing idle time; progress that accelerates near the end feels faster and stalls near 100% frustrate. Notifications on the web are viable but on iOS only for Home-Screen-installed web apps and only after a user gesture.

### Cited Findings
- NN/g: use a progress indicator for any action longer than about 1 s; looped animations for ~2–10 s; percent-done for ≥10 s; for >10 s show an estimate of completion. Spinners say the system is working but not how long. Replace static "Loading…" text. Add text stating the reason ("Loading comments…") and counters ("Updating address 3 of 50"). — [NN/g, Progress Indicators Make a Slow System Less Insufferable](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progress-indicators/)
- NN/g citing a University of Nebraska–Lincoln study: people shown a moving progress bar reported higher satisfaction and were willing to wait on average about 3× longer than with no indicator. — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progress-indicators/)
- NN/g on estimates: precise estimates "will inevitably be inaccurate at some point" and hurt credibility — give a rough estimate and pad it; start slower and speed up near the end; a bar that stalls near 100% frustrates; for long actions offer a way to stop. Knowing the wait lets users attend to other tasks (DMV callback example). — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progress-indicators/)
- Harrison, Amento, Kuznetsov & Bell, "Rethinking the Progress Bar" (UIST 2007): pairwise comparisons of bars with equal 5.5 s duration showed some progress behaviours perceptually speed up execution; per a secondary summary, bars that start slow and accelerate near the end were preferred. — [Chris Harrison, Progress Bars](https://www.chrisharrison.net/index.php/Research/ProgressBars); [OSNews summary](https://www.osnews.com/story/19430/rethinking-the-progress-bar/)
- Harrison, Yeo & Hudson, "Faster Progress Bars" (CHI 2010): visual augmentations make bars appear faster with unchanged actual duration. — [FIGLAB, Faster Progress Bars](https://www.figlab.com/research/2010/faster-progress-bars)
- Buell & Norton, "The Labor Illusion: How Operational Transparency Increases Perceived Value", Management Science 57(9):1564–1579 (2011): across five experiments (online travel and dating), people sometimes preferred sites with longer waits that showed the work being done (e.g. a running tally of results instead of a progress bar), even with identical results. — [SUFE academic newsletter summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/394153); [researchr bibliographic record](https://researchr.org/publication/BuellN11/bibtex)
- Bray (2023, reported as Manufacturing & Service Operations Management): true operational transparency reveals idleness as well as work, so its benefit depends on when work happens (linked to the peak-end effect). — [Bray, Operational transparency (PDF)](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/bray/doc/transparency/transparency.pdf)
- PAIR: explain the recovery plan ahead of time and in the moment when something goes wrong; address the current error. — [PAIR](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/)
- Apple HIG: "Ideally, your feature can recognize and describe the reasons for poor results"; "Suggest alternative ways to accomplish the goal instead of showing no results"; "Consider telling people when limitations are resolved." — [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- HAX G14 "Update and adapt cautiously — Limit disruptive changes"; G18 "Notify users about changes"; G3 "Time when to act or interrupt based on the user's current task and environment". — [Amershi et al. 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2019/01/Guidelines-for-Human-AI-Interaction-camera-ready.pdf)
- WebKit (Feb 2023): Web Push for Home Screen web apps from iOS/iPadOS 16.4; permission can only be requested in response to a direct user interaction (e.g. a subscribe button); sites in regular Safari tabs cannot request push on iOS; the Badging API is supported for Home Screen web apps; delivered via APNs, no Apple Developer membership needed. — [WebKit, Web Push for Web Apps on iOS and iPadOS](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/)
- Moises describes stem isolation as "submit your track and wait briefly"; no source found on its notification behaviour. — [Moises Hi-Fi Stem Separation](https://moises.ai/features/hi-fi-stem-separation)

### Inferences
- Splitune's DAG (`[url_import →] decode → {beats, tag} → separate → stem_post → {chords, notes.<instrument>} → finalize`) is a natural "operational transparency" story: each completed stage can show a *real* artefact (waveform after decode; BPM and ticking beat grid after beats; "heard: guitar, bass, keys" after tagging; stem waveforms after separation; first chords after chords). This applies the labor illusion honestly — real data, no fake activity.
- Bray's caveat means Splitune should not foreground idle time (e.g. "waiting for worker" for minutes in personal mode when the owner's Mac is asleep); state it once, plainly, with what will happen next, rather than a frozen bar.
- One overall determinate bar weighted by typical stage durations (separation dominates), plus a stage checklist with per-stage state (queued / running / done / skipped / failed), satisfies NN/g's "percent-done + reason" rule; ETA should be a padded range ("about 3–5 min") updated as stages complete, never a countdown in seconds.
- Progressive results are the biggest perceived-speed lever available: beats + full mix unlock loop/slow-down/count-in practice within the first stages; chords next; stems/mixer after separation; tab last. Each arrival should be announced unobtrusively and not reflow the score under the playhead (G14), e.g. "Tab is ready — Show".
- Hard vs soft dependency failures map to honest partial results: if `notes.bass` fails, the song still opens with chords and other parts and a plain-language reason plus Retry / Try another source (Apple "suggest alternative ways").
- Leave-and-return: processing must survive navigation and tab close (state lives in Postgres jobs + SSE), with library cards showing stage state, an in-app toast on completion, document-title/badge updates, and optional Web Push requested only when the user clicks "Notify me when ready" (WebKit gesture rule; iOS only when installed as a PWA). Email is not available in personal mode (no mail server per CLAUDE.md).
- Cancel should be available at every stage and, for re-analysis of a single stage, should restore the previous result.

### Gaps
- No primary sources retrieved for Descript, LALAL.AI, Runway/Midjourney queues, Vercel deploy logs or GitHub Actions UX; their patterns (stage logs, queue position, per-step timing) are known from experience but unverified here.
- No study found specifically on progressive/partial results in ML media pipelines and their effect on perceived wait or trust.
- Effect sizes for Buell & Norton were not retrieved; current status of iOS Web Push in the EU (after Apple's 2024 iOS 17.4 Home Screen web app changes) was not verified.
- An ethics critique of the labor illusion ("ethically deceptive design") surfaced in search but was not read.

## 5. How should expectations be set and trust calibrated (strengths/limits, avoiding over-trust, jargon-free explanation, onboarding)?

### Takeaway
Tell users up front what the system is good and bad at (in benefit language, no jargon), give a low-commitment sandbox, attach reasons to poor results at the moment they appear, and keep explanations partial and actionable. "How well it works" (HAX G2) and "why" (G11) are the most commonly neglected guidelines.

### Cited Findings
- HAX G1/G2: make clear what the system can do and "how often the AI system may make mistakes"; G2 and G11 were among the relevant-but-least-implemented guidelines in the CHI 2019 study; a participant cited hedging language ("we think you'll like") as an application of G2. — [Amershi et al. 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2019/01/Guidelines-for-Human-AI-Interaction-camera-ready.pdf)
- PAIR onboarding: "Focus on the benefit, not the technology"; set expectations for capabilities and limits early; offer a low-commitment "sandbox"; admit data gaps (e.g. "Be careful. It's after 6pm and our run route recommendations don't include street light data"); "The best explanation is likely a partial one"; skip explanations where behaviour matches common mental models. — [PAIR](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/)
- PAIR: tie explanations to user actions — "People learn faster when they can see a response to their actions right away." — [PAIR](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/)
- Apple HIG: "When a limitation may have a serious effect on user experience but happens rarely, consider making people aware of the limitation before they use your app or feature"; attributions express the basis for a result "without explaining exactly how a model works"; "avoid technical or statistical jargon". — [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- Zhang et al.: confidence display improved trust calibration but not accuracy; humans need complementary knowledge to catch AI errors. — [arXiv 2001.02114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02114v1)
- Songscription's own guidance: the recording environment affects transcription accuracy more than any other variable. — [Songscription blog](https://www.songscription.ai/blog/accurate-ai-music-transcriptions)

### Inferences
- For learners, the "complementary knowledge" is their ear: confidence marks should invite listening ("loop this bar slowly and check") rather than ask for abstract judgement, turning uncertainty into a practice action.
- A short "Works best with / Has trouble with" panel at import (clear studio recordings and isolated parts vs distorted, dense, live or lo-fi recordings; fast passages; alternate tunings) applies G1/G2 and Apple's "rare but serious limitation" rule; repeat it contextually when the song-level gate fires, naming the likely cause.
- A pre-analysed demo song (sandbox) that contains some "?" marks and a guided "fix one chord" moment would teach the confidence vocabulary and correction flow before the user's first multi-minute wait.

### Gaps
- No onboarding study specific to music-transcription apps found.
- Bansal et al.-style evidence that explanations can increase over-reliance regardless of correctness was not retrieved in this session.

## 6. How should progress and confidence be made accessible (aria-live, non-colour encoding)?

### Takeaway
Use a polite live region that already exists in the DOM, announce milestones not ticks, batch updates, and reserve assertive/alert for failures that need action; encode confidence and edit provenance in accessible names, not only visually.

### Cited Findings
- MDN: `aria-live="polite"` announces at the next graceful opportunity; assertive interrupts. — [MDN, aria-live](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/Accessibility/ARIA/Attributes/aria-live)
- Live regions used with high-frequency updates create barriers; be mindful of update frequency. — [Toronto Metropolitan University, Web Accessibility for Developers: live regions](https://pressbooks.library.torontomu.ca/wafd/chapter/live-regions/)
- Screen reader support for live regions varies across browser/AT combinations (source is older). — [Léonie Watson, tink.uk](https://tink.uk/screen-reader-support-for-aria-live-regions/); [a11ysupport.io, aria-live](https://a11ysupport.io/tech/aria/aria-live_attribute)
- Splitune already uses `aria-live="polite"` for the transient PedalHud label and requires confidence never be shown by colour alone. — `docs/specs/26-design-system.md`; `CLAUDE.md` engineering rules

### Inferences
- Announce one sentence per stage transition ("Separating instruments, step 3 of 6, about 3 minutes left"), plus partial-result arrivals ("Chords are ready"); never announce percentage ticks. Use `role="progressbar"` with `aria-valuetext` carrying the stage text for users who query it.
- Failures that block the user's goal can use `role="alert"`; soft failures that still leave a usable song should stay polite.
- Accessible names should carry the same three-level vocabulary as the visuals ("A minor, unsure; alternatives: C major, A minor 7") and the provenance ("edited by you").
- Provide a keyboard/pedal command "next unsure item" so screen-reader and pedal users can review flags without visual scanning.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for progress animation and HOPs-style animated uncertainty (if ever used).

### Gaps
- WCAG 2.2 SC 4.1.3 Status Messages (AA) almost certainly applies to processing status but its text was not fetched in this session.
- No evidence found on how screen-reader users prefer to consume per-note confidence in tab/notation.

## 7. Concrete patterns and recommendations for Splitune

### Takeaway
Keep Splitune's existing non-hue three-level confidence system and song-level gate, but (1) flag fewer, more consequential items, (2) make every flag a one-step guided correction with audible verification, (3) make corrections cascade through music-core recomputation with visible provenance and undo, and (4) turn the processing wait into a progressive, operationally transparent stage timeline that users can leave and return to.

### Cited Findings
- Evidence for each item is in sections 1–6: [PAIR](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/), [Amershi et al. 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2019/01/Guidelines-for-Human-AI-Interaction-camera-ready.pdf), [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning), [Vasconcelos et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07248v3), [Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02114v1), [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progress-indicators/), [Buell & Norton](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/394153), [Bray](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/bray/doc/transparency/transparency.pdf), [Harrison et al.](https://www.chrisharrison.net/index.php/Research/ProgressBars), [Melodyne](https://helpcenter.celemony.com/M5/doc/melodyneEditor5/en/M5tour_NA_Mode_1?env=live), [Logic Smart Tempo](https://help.apple.com/logicpro/mac/10.4/en.lproj/lgcp9281e70c.html), [Klangio Retranscribe](https://klang.io/?p=10950), [WebKit Web Push](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/), [MDN aria-live](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/Accessibility/ARIA/Attributes/aria-live).

### Inferences
1. **Keep three named levels, no numbers.** Sure / likely / unsure, encoded by ink value + dashed outline + "?" (doc 26), with the same words in a one-tap legend and in accessible names. No percentages in the practice UI (PAIR numeric risks; Apple semantic categories).
2. **Show confidence only where it is calibrated.** Check reliability per stage, instrument and band on the eval set before surfacing; if chords or beats are uncalibrated, show no marks for that stage (Apple).
3. **Flag what needs fixing, not what the model doubts.** Rank flags by likely-and-consequential error (pitch/fret, octave slips, chord root/quality), ideally learned from stored correction overlays, not raw posteriors (Vasconcelos).
4. **Cap flag density.** When most of a bar is unsure, mark the bar ("shaky bar") instead of every note; offer a global "Show unsure marks" toggle (HAX G17; "not overwhelming").
5. **Phrase the song-level gate in frequencies and causes.** Keep the 0.80 Chart fallback and "Show notes anyway", but write "About 1 in 4 notes may be off — the guitar is buried under distortion" and offer alternatives such as chords, slower loop or another stem (frequency framing; Apple attributions, limitations and alternatives).
6. **Use N-best chord chips.** Tapping an unsure chord shows 2–3 alternatives, most likely first and audibly different, each auditionable over the current loop, plus "Other…" and "Simplify to triad" (PAIR N-best; Apple multiple options; Moises levels).
7. **Add a separate Fix mode for notes and tab.** Practice view shows only marks. Fix mode reveals candidate alternatives as hollow outlines (Melodyne silhouettes) and guided one-key fixes: octave ±, same pitch on another string, delete or merge, and "jump to next unsure", all keyboard and pedal operable.
8. **Let beat and downbeat fixes cascade.** Offer "This is beat 1", "Shift bars by one beat" and tap or click hints (Logic Smart Tempo). Re-quantize chords and tab instantly in the browser with music-core, preview the consequence ("Re-barred 84 bars") and make it undoable (Apple "immediate value"; HAX G16).
9. **Provide Retranscribe with corrected inputs.** Let users fix key, tuning, capo, tempo or instrument tag and re-run only the affected downstream stages, showing which stages will run and a rough time (Klangio Retranscribe; the CLAUDE.md rule that quantization is a separate re-runnable stage).
10. **Make provenance visible and reversible.** Mark user-edited items with a non-colour "edited" glyph, offer per-item "Revert to transcription", a global "Show original" compare toggle and full undo/redo. Re-analysis never discards overlays (the anti-pattern is Melodyne's algorithm change); conflicts are flagged for review.
11. **Don't over-promise learning.** Say "Your fixes are saved for this song". Don't imply the model learns from them (HAX G2/G13 honesty) and don't nag for ratings: corrections are the feedback (Apple voluntary explicit feedback).
12. **Build an honest stage timeline.** Show one determinate overall bar weighted by typical stage cost, plus a stage checklist (queued, running, done, skipped, failed) with plain names ("Finding the beat", "Separating instruments", "Writing the tab"). Give a padded ETA range that updates per stage, accelerates near the end and never stalls at 99% (NN/g; Harrison).
13. **Show real work as it happens.** Each finished stage shows its artefact: waveform, BPM with a ticking grid, detected instruments, stem waveforms, first chords. Mention idle or queue time once, honestly, with the next step, rather than as a frozen bar (Buell & Norton; Bray).
14. **Unlock practice progressively.** Open the song once decode and beats are done (loop, slow-down and count-in on the full mix), then add chords, then the stem mixer, then tab. Each arrival gets a quiet "Tab is ready — Show" notice that never reflows the score under the playhead (HAX G14/G18).
15. **Let users leave and come back.** Processing survives navigation, library cards show the stage state, and completion triggers an in-app toast plus a title/badge update. Request Web Push only when the user clicks "Notify me when ready"; on iOS it works only as an installed PWA (WebKit).
16. **Treat failure as a partial result.** A soft-dependency failure still opens the song with what exists, gives the reason in plain words and offers Retry / Try another source / Practise with chords. Automatic retries are shown as "Retrying (2 of 3)". Cancel is available at every stage, and cancelling a re-run restores the previous result (PAIR recovery plan; Apple alternatives).
17. **Set expectations at import and with a sandbox.** Show a short "Works best with / Has trouble with" list plus input-quality hints before upload. A demo song with a few "?" marks and a guided "fix one chord" moment teaches the vocabulary before the first long wait (HAX G1/G2; PAIR sandbox; Songscription's recording-quality guidance).
18. **Make all of it accessible.** One pre-existing polite live region announces stage milestones and partial-result arrivals, never percentage ticks. Use `role="progressbar"` with `aria-valuetext`, and `role="alert"` only for blocking failures. Accessible names carry the confidence level, alternatives and "edited by you", and progress animations respect reduced motion (MDN; doc 26).

### Gaps
- Recommendations 3, 4, 10 (conflict handling) and 14 are design inferences without direct experimental evidence in music apps; they should be validated with PAIR-style trust probes and task-time tests on Splitune's own fixtures.
