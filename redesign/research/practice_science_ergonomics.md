# Practice science and practising ergonomics for the Splitune practice screen

Scope: evidence on effective instrument practice, motivation, hands-busy input, physical setup, accessibility and instrument-specific needs, turned into testable UX requirements. Researched 2026-11-08. This builds on three existing repo notes, which the report writer should treat as prior work: `docs/research/ux/learning-science-principles.md` (P1–P10, 2026-10-20), `docs/research/ux/hands-busy-and-device-context.md` and `docs/specs/12-practice-screen-and-notation.md` §5 and §8 (current loop, speed and pedal defaults). Where I re-use a source those notes cite and did not re-open it in this session, I say so.

Evidence-strength labels used below: **MA** = meta-analysis or systematic review; **EXP** = controlled experiment (sample size given); **OBS** = observational or correlational study; **QUAL** = qualitative, survey or thesis; **DOC** = vendor or standards documentation; **OP** = practitioner opinion, blog or forum.

## 1. Practice science: which practice behaviours are evidenced, and which can an app scaffold?

### Takeaway
The best-supported finding is that how you practise matters more than how long. Strong practisers find the exact error, fix it at once (often by slowing just before the trouble spot) and repeat the section until it stays correct across several trials. Segmenting, slowing and whole-part-whole are the observed behaviours that correlate with achievement. Evidence for specific tempo-ladder shapes, interleaving, metronome use, mental practice and play-along is thin: small samples, mixed or null results. An app can scaffold the well-supported behaviours (bounded loops, error marking, repeat-until-stable, tempo control, rest between passes, next-day review) and should ship the weakly supported ones as options with in-product measurement, not as fixed rules.

### Cited Findings

**Deliberate practice: amount vs quality**
- (MA) A meta-analysis of 88 studies found deliberate practice explained 21% of variance in music performance (r ≈ .46): games 26%, sports 18%, education 4%, professions under 1%. The overall figure was 12% (r = .35) — [Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald 2014 (record)](https://stafforini.com/works/macnamara-2014-deliberate-practice-and/); [PMC commentary "Facing facts about deliberate practice"](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4101876/)
- (MA) A music-only meta-analysis of 13 studies found a corrected correlation of rc = 0.61 between deliberate practice and musical achievement, so the share of variance explained in music is disputed (roughly 21% to about a third) — [Platz, Kopiez, Lehmann & Wolf 2014, Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00646/pdf); [reply commentary](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00751/full)
- (EXP) A double-blind replication of the classic violinist study found practice explained about 26% of variance, against 48% in the 1993 original — [Macnamara & Maitra 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6731745/) (cited in the existing repo note; not re-opened)

**Error correction and "practise the hard bars" (Duke, Simmons & Cash 2009)**
- (OBS, n = 17) Seventeen graduate and advanced undergraduate piano majors at UT Austin practised a three-measure Shostakovich passage (Piano Concerto No. 1) and were tested the next day. Strategy, not practice time, predicted performance — [JRME 56(4), 310–321, DOI 10.1177/0022429408328851](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022429408328851); [secondary summary, Portuguese paper](https://www.eca.usp.br/acervo/producao-academica/002984292.pdf)
- (OBS) The top performers did not make fewer errors at first. They dealt with them differently: after an error they slowed down or hesitated, without stopping, just before the spot where they had gone wrong. Target passages were repeated until the error was corrected and the passage stayed stable across later trials. The top 8 had a mean of 3 incorrect trials against 13.56 for the bottom 9 — [unofficial copy of the article (Scribd); verify against the journal](https://www.scribd.com/document/494219936/DukeEtAl2)
- (OBS) The existing repo note records that, per the abstract, stronger players also changed tempo deliberately (slowing to fix, speeding up to test) — [docs/research/ux/learning-science-principles.md, citing the journal abstract via a search excerpt](https://www.structural-learning.com/research-briefings/practise-the-hard-bit)

**Observed practice behaviours that correlate with achievement (Miksza)**
- (OBS, n = 60) High-school wind players were observed while practising. The most frequent behaviours were repeat measure, repeat section and marking the part. Performance achievement correlated significantly with repeat section, whole-part-whole, slowing, and skipping directly to (or just before) a passage — [Miksza, "Effective practice", JRME (abstract)](https://www.sciencegate.app/document/10.1177/0022429408317513). I read only the abstract, and the DOI shown on that listing may belong to a different Miksza paper, so check it.
- (MA) A PRISMA systematic review of music-practice research from 1928 to June 2020 screened 3,102 records and kept 296. Self-regulation, deliberate practice, practice strategies and metacognition were among the most common topics — [How, Tan & Miksza, PRISMA review (aggregator record)](https://bohrium.dp.tech/paper/arxiv/864984283045953801)
- (QUAL, n = 3,325) A survey of young people, from beginners to conservatoire-entry level, found seven practising factors. They include systematic practice strategies, organisation of practice, use of recordings for listening and feedback together with use of the metronome, analytic strategies, and ineffective strategies. Systematic strategies grew with expertise — [Hallam et al. 2012, Psychology of Music 40(5)](https://pure.unic.ac.cy/en/publications/the-development-of-practising-strategies-in-young-people/)

**Slow practice and tempo ladders**
- (EXP, n = 60) High-school wind players practised an étude six times under combinations of modelling (model vs none) and tempo pattern: a steady increase, performance tempo throughout, or alternating slower and faster. The model improved rhythm and tempo gains. **The tempo pattern had no significant effect** — [Henley 2001, JRME 49(2) 169–180](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2307/3345799); [1999 LSU dissertation listing the conditions](https://repository.lsu.edu/gradschool_disstheses/6992)
- (OP) Kageyama's Bulletproof Musician argues that slow-only practice can backfire. Its cited study, on throwing rather than music, found the speed-emphasis group did better on a later speed-plus-accuracy test — [Bulletproof Musician, Jason Sulliman interview](https://bulletproofmusician.com/jason-sulliman-on-why-fast-practice-can-be-more-efficient-and-effective-than-slow-practice/)
- (OP) A related piece argues that heavy slowing changes the task into an "ordinal" one (note order without timing), so the timing has to be re-learned as tempo rises — [Soundfly Flypaper](https://flypaper.soundfly.com/play/why-slow-accurate-practice-may-actually-be-the-problem-2/)
- (DOC) Competitor precedent for a tempo ladder: Soundslice speed training (start and end speed, step, plays per speed; example 50%→100% in 10% steps, 4 plays each) and Guitar Pro 8 Progressive Speed (10–300%) — [Soundslice speed training](https://www.soundslice.com/help/en/player/basic/286/speed-training/); [Guitar Pro 8 speed](https://www.guitar-pro.com/docs/gp8/audio/playback/speed) (cited in the existing repo note)

**Blocked vs interleaved (contextual interference)**
- (EXP, n = 10, within-subjects) Ten advanced clarinetists practised a concerto exposition and a technical study blocked (12 min each) and another pair interleaved (3-minute alternation to 12 min each), then had a retention test one day later. Averaged across raters interleaved always scored higher, but not significantly; one single-rater result reached p = .02; inter-rater r ran from 0.02 to 0.59. Blocked technical pieces declined from Day 1 to Day 2 — [Carter & Grahn 2016, Frontiers in Psychology (PMC full text)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4989027/)
- (EXP, same paper) All 10 said their normal practice was blocked. Six found interleaving more useful, but six preferred blocked. All 8 who commented said interleaving helped goal setting, focus and spotting mistakes ("shorter segments required more focus"; switching helped when stuck) — [Carter & Grahn 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4989027/)
- (EXP, summarised in Carter & Grahn) In Stambaugh 2009, beginner clarinetists in grades 5–6 played faster at retention after interleaved practice. Stambaugh & Demorest 2010 (grade 7 clarinet and sax) found no schedule effect on accuracy. In Stambaugh 2011a (university players) the effect held for woodwinds but not brass — [Carter & Grahn 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4989027/)

**Spacing, rest and sleep consolidation (repertoire review)**
- (EXP, n = 29) Non-pianist musicians learned a 9-note keyboard sequence in three sessions separated by 5 min, 6 h or 24 h. The authors concluded that sleep-based consolidation may improve accuracy, while speed gains come from both wake and sleep consolidation — [Simmons 2011, JRME 59(4) 357–368 (record)](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/distributed-practice-and-procedural-memory-consolidation-in-musicians-skill-learning/813362932149649409-5172); [UT CML research page](https://cml.music.utexas.edu/research/skill-learning-and-procedural-memory)
- (EXP) Simmons & Duke 2006 had trained musicians who were not pianists learn a 12-note melody with the non-dominant hand. A secondary source describes it as an early demonstration of sleep-based improvement in a musical motor skill. I did not obtain the effect sizes — [JRME 54(3) 257–269, JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/4151346)
- (MA) A general motor and verbal meta-analysis (63 studies) found a mean weighted effect of 0.46 in favour of spaced over massed practice — [Donovan & Radosevich 1999](https://www.gwern.net/docs/spaced-repetition/1999-donovan.pdf) (cited in the existing repo note; not re-opened)

**Mental practice**
- (EXP, n = 50) Trumpet students were assigned to physical practice, mental imagery, overt singing, all three combined, or a no-practice control. Physical and combined practice beat no practice on overall performance and pitch accuracy. Only the combined condition significantly improved musical expression. The authors suggest imagery and singing can cut the amount of physical practice needed without losing quality. This was short-term learning of unfamiliar pieces — [Steenstrup et al. 2021, Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.757052/full)
- (EXP) The same paper notes that Miksza et al. (2018) did not find mental practice useful for learning jazz improvisation — [Steenstrup et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.757052/full)

**Thinking time, self-recording and self-evaluation**
- (OBS, n = 32) More experienced players spent at least as long imagining as playing and paused more between trials. School-age players rarely paused and often worked on issues other than the ones they had identified — [Palese & Duke 2022 (PDF)](https://cml.music.utexas.edu/sites/default/files/documents/paleseduke2022.pdf) (cited in the existing repo note)
- (EXP, n = 26) Listening to oneself before rating had no significant effect on how accurately undergraduates self-evaluated — [Effect of self-listening on self-evaluation accuracy](https://digitalcommons.memphis.edu/facpubs/68)
- (EXP, n ≈ 25) With advanced piano students, replay of their own playing (Disklavier) changed self-ratings on some parameters, but external ratings showed no significant effect — [Frontiers in Psychology 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1568021/full)
- (OP, summarising Silveira & Gavin 2015) Middle-school players sounded different on playback than they expected, and a delay of a day or two before listening may make judgement more objective. Performing and critiquing at the same moment is hard — [Bulletproof Musician](https://bulletproofmusician.com/evidence-that-youre-probably-not-great-at-performing-and-critiquing-your-performance-at-the-same-time/)

**Play-along with band or stems vs isolated practice**
- (EXP, secondary report) Juntunen, Ruokonen & Ruismäki (2014) had conservatoire string players learn a piece either against an orchestral backing track or from notation alone. Playing with the recording significantly improved emotional connection with the music, provided the backing was of good quality. I could not check the original paper — [Music Teacher magazine, "Backing tracks: good or bad?"](https://musicteachermagazine.co.uk/content/feature/backing-tracks-good-or-bad)
- (OP) Teacher and exam-board guidance recommends rehearsing with the accompaniment before performing and listening to the track first, so there are no surprises — [NFHS: live and recorded accompaniment](https://nfhs.org/stories/live-and-recorded-accompaniment-both-have-benefits-for-the-musician); [Trinity College digital-exam accompaniment requirements](https://support.trinitycollege.com/support/solutions/articles/202000062425-music-digital-requirements-accompaniment-and-backing-tracks)
- I found no study comparing reduced-mix with full-mix practice (see Gaps).

**Metronome vs groove**
- (EXP, thesis) Undergraduate pianists: metronome practice at 60 BPM produced no significant differences in pitch, rhythm or beat-consistency accuracy compared with other strategies — [Hanberry 2004, LSU dissertation](https://repository.lsu.edu/gradschool_dissertations/1187/)
- (EXP, n = 19, thesis) A rhythm-analysis device was compared with a metronome on snare exercises. Two of five exercises showed large effects favouring the analysis device, which suggests feedback on your own timing may matter more than the click — [Klausing 2010, Louisville](https://ir.library.louisville.edu/etd/761)
- (EXP, tapping study) With a continuous metronome, its type did not affect inter-onset intervals. Removing the metronome for a number of beats did significantly affect IOI consistency, which supports "gap click" drills that test the internal pulse — [Owens & Cunningham, "More Cowbell"](https://wrexham.repository.guildhe.ac.uk/id/eprint/17318/1/More%20Cowbell_Variations_inc%20c%20sheet.pdf) (abstract-level only)

**Feedback**
- (MA/EXP mixed) Under the "guidance hypothesis", frequent augmented feedback can create dependence. Replications are inconsistent, and Wulf & Shea (2004) found "little evidence" that feedback on every trial causes dependence — [Frontiers in Psychology 2010](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2010.00190/full) (cited in the existing repo note)

### Inferences
- The behaviours with the best support (bounded section, pinpointed error, slowing just before the error, repeat until stable, whole-part-whole, skipping directly to the passage) all reduce to one interaction: **jump to an exact bar and loop it, at an adjustable tempo, with almost no setup cost.** The "skip directly to the passage" correlate is essentially a navigation-speed requirement.
- Carter & Grahn's Day 1 to Day 2 decline for blocked technical work, together with the sleep-consolidation studies, implies that **in-session performance is a misleading progress metric.** The honest retention measure is the first clean pass on the next day. This also guards against learners mistaking blocked-practice fluency for learning.
- Henley's null result on tempo pattern means the tempo-ladder shape (step size, clean passes per step) is not evidence-derived. Make it a user-adjustable default and A/B test it; a fixed curve should not be marketed as "science".
- Duke's "slow just before the error" is a concrete, novel scaffold: the tempo dips only in the bar before a marked trouble spot rather than across the whole loop. It is directly modelled on expert behaviour but has never been tested as an app feature.
- The combined imagine, sing and play result supports a cheap "listen-through first" pass (hear the isolated part once without playing, then play). It costs almost nothing in an app that already isolates the part.
- Juntunen points to play-along mainly helping musical expression, not accuracy. So a sensible progression is isolated or "your part loud" for accuracy work, then full band or minus-one for musicality and run-throughs.

### Gaps
- Full text of Duke et al. 2009 is still unverified (journal 403; only an unofficial copy and summaries). I could not confirm the exact list of the "three strategies the top pianists used".
- I found no music-specific controlled study of starting percentage (60–70%) or step size for tempo ladders. The Allingham & Wöllner (2022) qualitative slow-practice study and Kerr & Booth (1978) appear only in secondary sources. Kerr & Booth was a bean-bag-throwing variability study, so applying it to tempo is an extrapolation.
- I found no study of reduced-mix vs full-mix practice, or of stem-mixer practice in general.
- Spaced repetition for repertoire (review schedules over days or weeks) has no music-specific trial I could find; only consolidation studies of 5 min to 24 h on 9- to 12-note sequences.
- Arthur, Khuu & Blom (2016), "Is the metronome harmful or helpful?", was not retrieved.
- Nearly all studies use piano, woodwinds or trumpet; I found none on guitar, bass or drums.

## 2. Motivation and habit: self-regulation, goals, logs, streaks, gamification, adult hobbyists

### Takeaway
Self-regulation can be taught and helps (the strategies it names are planning, proximal goals, monitoring and reflection). Self-reports of practice are unreliable, so logs should be automatic, not typed. Gamification in music apps shows motivational benefit only in small studies, with no evidence of skill gains. Streaks drive consistency but create loss frustration. Adult hobbyists judge success by enjoyment and drop out at high (company-reported) rates, which argues for low-friction "continue my song" sessions and informational rather than controlling feedback.

### Cited Findings
- (EXP) In Miksza (2013), advanced wind players trained in practice techniques plus self-regulation strategies improved significantly more on an étude by day five than those trained in techniques alone — [secondary summary](https://blog.sonicbids.com/research-tested-practice-strategies-that-will-help-you-learn-new-pieces-faster) (secondary source; original in Psychology of Music)
- (OBS) A self-regulated-learning microanalysis protocol (McPherson, Osborne, Evans, Miksza 2019) maps behaviours, thoughts and feelings in practice. A follow-up used a three-phase SRL practice diary to see whether self-regulation increased over a semester — [University of Melbourne record](https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/scholarlywork/1270108-applying-self-regulated-learning-microanalysis-to-study-musicians%E2%80%99-practice)
- (OBS) Subgroup differences in **self-reported** self-regulated practice repeatedly conflicted with **observed** behaviour — [Holmes-Davis 2015, "But I did practice!"](https://open.bu.edu/items/6a4e02ef-3c43-4cdc-b1a3-d446f5491900)
- (QUAL, n = 52, thesis) A structured practice journal helped middle-school string students set proximal personal and musical goals and use more self-regulatory strategies — [Indiana University thesis](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/dspace/items/dce1cb2f-648e-4a16-821a-766175e818cf)
- (OP/review) Case studies of expert practice show experts organise practice around small, achievable goals. Novices' goals are often too broad and focused on accuracy alone — [MTNA e-Journal 2024](https://certification.mtna.org/downloads/Informed/EJ/AOY/2024_E-Journal_AOY.pdf)
- (OBS) Self-efficacy was the strongest predictor of achievement in graded music exams — [McPherson & McCormick 2006 (unofficial copy)](https://www.scribd.com/document/372783314/Mcpherson-Mccormick-2006)
- (QUAL) Gamified guitar-learning software showed motivation-enhancing effects in a single implementation study — [Turkish Journal of Education 2023](https://avesis.marmara.edu.tr/yayin/cd635138-10da-4e35-a8f6-8b9f5a0ae379/a-study-on-educational-software-development-through-gamification-in-guitar-education)
- (QUAL, thesis) A browser guitar-tuition app found gamification appeared to raise engagement and prevent boredom. The author called the results inconclusive and noted that negative feedback hurt motivation — [Grigutis 2018, University of Huddersfield](https://eprints.hud.ac.uk/id/eprint/34633)
- (MA) General gamification reviews: effects lean positive but depend on context and user, may be novelty effects, and removing gamification can hurt users who were engaged by it (loss aversion) — [Hamari, Koivisto & Sarsa 2014](https://www.lescahiersdelinnovation.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/2014-hamari_et_al-does_gamification_work.pdf) (cited in the existing repo note)
- (QUAL, n = 7) Duolingo streaks mainly drove consistency, but participants reported frustration from losing streaks and from competitive pressure — [UMPO thesis](https://eprints.umpo.ac.id/21371/)
- (OP/conceptual) Self-determination theory applied to music education argues that extrinsic rewards for practising tend to undermine intrinsic motivation, citing Deci, Koestner & Ryan 2001 — [Evans 2015, Musicae Scientiae](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/2015_Evans_SDT_MusicEdu.pdf) (cited in the existing repo note)
- (company claim) Fender's CEO said 45% of Fender guitars sold in a year go to new players and that 90% of beginners abandon within 12 months. He also said that cutting abandonment by 10% could double the industry. This comes from Fender customer surveys, not a published study — [Guitar World](https://www.guitarworld.com/news/90-percent-of-new-guitarists-abandon-the-instrument-within-a-year-according-to-fender); [Fortune 2022](https://fortune.com/2022/10/21/guitar-industry-fender-ceo-credits-diversity-players-pandemic-revival); skepticism about the method on the [JustinGuitar community](https://community.justinguitar.com/t/interesting-perspectives-from-the-ceo-of-fender/179132)
- (survey) In ABRSM's Making Music 2021 (UK; surveys largely from 2020), 13% of adults make music digitally, fewer than 25% of learners progress from school group lessons to formal lessons, and 71% of teachers cite cost as a barrier — [Music Teacher magazine on ABRSM 2021](https://www.musicteachermagazine.co.uk/articles/abrsm-issues-warning-over-music-teacher-numbers-in-new-report)
- (survey) A Making Music slide citing ABRSM 2017–18 data says 13% of ABRSM exams were taken by adults. It also lists "63% retired" and "75% have a teacher", with the base unclear — [Making Music: Adult leisure-time music, the stats (PDF)](https://makingmusic.org.uk/sites/makingmusic.org.uk/files/Adult%20leisure-time%20music%20-%20the%20stats.pdf)
- (QUAL) Adult amateur learners define success by enjoyment, and 61% never intend to monetise their music (informal survey) — [AYO: insight into adult learners](https://ayo.com.au/why-am-i-doing-this-an-insight-into-the-experiences-of-adult-learners-of-musical-instruments/)
- (survey, mixed population) Learning something new at least somewhat motivated over 94% of respondents to practise, and 73% were motivated by others' encouragement — [Virtual Sheet Music practice-motivation survey](https://virtualsheetmusic.com/blog/motivation-to-practice)
- (QUAL, n = 10) Well-being is an important motivator for adult hobby musicians to stay committed — [University of Jyväskylä thesis](https://jyx.jyu.fi/jyx/Record/jyx_123456789_97799)

### Inferences
- Because self-reports diverge from what learners actually do (Holmes-Davis), Splitune should **log automatically** what was looped, at what tempo, and whether passes were judged clean. Ask only one-tap reflection questions ("felt clean / not yet", already in doc 12 §5).
- Proximal goals should be **generated from data and editable**, for example "bars 17–20 clean at 80%" rather than "practise 20 min". That matches expert behaviour and the clean-pass metric already in doc 12 (`best_clean_tempo_pct`).
- Streaks, if any, should be forgiving (weekly targets, "freeze" days, no loss animation) and never the main progress display. Competence feedback (tempo reached, clean passes) is the SDT-aligned alternative.
- For time-poor adult hobbyists, the dominant lever is **time-to-playing**: reopening the last song at the last loop and last clean tempo in one action. This inference rests on the high abandonment claim and on enjoyment-defined success; no direct study.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous study of streak mechanics in music apps, or of Yousician, Fender Play or Simply Piano retention mechanics (no public data).
- I found no representative survey of adult hobbyist practice time per week or of motivations specific to song learning. The Fender data are company claims. A small study reportedly found Rocksmith users treated it more as a game than a learning tool, but I could not retrieve its source.
- The 2025 ukulele gamification-and-practice study (Frontiers in Education) surfaced, but I did not obtain its findings.

## 3. Hands-busy interaction: pedals, MIDI, keyboard, voice, face gestures, touch

### Takeaway
Bluetooth page turners are HID keyboards whose keys depend on a hardware mode. AirTurn's default (Mode 2) sends Up and Down arrows. iRig BlueTurn offers Page Up/Down, Up/Down or Left/Right, chosen at power-up. Musicians expect pedals to "just work" and to be remappable. Pro apps (forScore) remap keyboard, MIDI, pedal and Pencil inputs in one panel. MIDI foot controllers cannot reach a web app on Safari (no Web MIDI). Face gestures and voice are niche and unreliable. Every action needs immediate visible confirmation (under about 100 ms). Audio monitoring paths should stay under about 25 ms to keep ensemble timing.

### Cited Findings
- (DOC) The AirTurn BT200S has seven modes, and each mode sends a different signal per pedal. **Mode 2 is the factory default.** The mode list:
  - Mode 1 = AirDirect (app-specific; avoids the on-screen keyboard)
  - Mode 2 = Up/Down arrows (plus Left/Right on 4-pedal units)
  - Mode 3 = Page Up/Page Down (plus Left/Right)
  - Mode 4 = media keys (play/pause, next/previous track, volume)
  - Mode 5 = Space/Enter and others
  - Mode 6 = F12, ], 1, X, Z, Shift+Z
  - Mode 7 = MIDI sustain-pedal functions (damper, sostenuto, soft, legato)

  The extracted tables differ between the BT200 and BT200S manuals — [AirTurn BT200S series manual (ManualsLib, p.13)](https://manualslib.com/manual/1986862/Airturn-Bt200s-Series.html?page=13); [BT200S-2 manual (Full Compass)](https://www.fullcompass.com/common/files/35397-BT200S2UserManual.pdf); [BT200 manual (McGill)](https://www.mcgill.ca/library/files/library/bt200_manual.pdf)
- (DOC, conflict) AirTurn's AirDirect developer FAQ instead lists Mode 3 = Left/Right and Mode 4 = Page Up/Down — [AirDirect FAQ](https://dev.airturn.com/sdk/docs/main/airdirect-faq.html) (cited in the existing repo note). Both sources agree that **Mode 2 = Up/Down arrows**.
- (DOC) The iRig BlueTurn has two silent, backlit footswitches over Bluetooth LE HID, with three modes (Page Up/Down, Arrow Up/Down, Arrow Left/Right) set from the hardware at power-up — [retailer product page](https://www.musicjunction.com.au/products/irig-blueturn-bluetooth-page-turner)
- (DOC) forScore's "Page Turners & Shortcuts" panel maps keyboard shortcuts, MIDI signals, page-turner presses and Apple Pencil 2 double-tap to actions, and most shortcuts can be remapped. Holding Cmd on an iPad keyboard shows the available shortcuts — [forScore: Page Turners & Shortcuts](https://forscore.co/?p=1641); [Default keyboard shortcuts](https://forscore.co/?p=1285)
- (DOC) forScore face gestures (mouth movement by default; head turns or winks optional; calibrated sensitivity) need a Pro subscription and a facial-tracking device. AirPods head-turn page turning exists from forScore 12.0.4 and cannot be customised — [forScore: Face gestures](https://forscore.co/face-gestures/)
- (OP) User reports on face gestures are mixed: "never quite worked" for one user, who preferred a pedal; another found them a little too sensitive — [Delcamp classical guitar forum](https://www.classicalguitardelcamp.com/viewtopic.php?p=1687299)
- (DOC) Web MIDI is unsupported in Safari on every version, including Technology Preview. Chrome 43+ and Firefox 108+ support it. The WebKit tracking bug has been open since 2013 — [caniuse: Web MIDI](https://caniuse.com/midi); [WebKit bug 107250](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=107250)
- (DOC) MDN marks `SpeechRecognition` as limited availability, and in Chrome it sends audio to a server — [MDN SpeechRecognition](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/SpeechRecognition) (cited in the existing repo note)
- (DOC/standard) Roughly 0.1 s is the limit for a response to feel instantaneous; up to 1 s keeps the user's flow — [Nielsen Norman Group: Response times, the 3 important limits](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/) (canonical source, not re-opened this session)
- (EXP) In networked clapping, 24 pairs with one-way delays of 3–78 ms: performance felt natural at about 10–25 ms, delays over 25 ms produced progressive tempo deceleration, and very short delays (under about 11.5 ms) produced slight acceleration — [Chafe & Gurevich 2004 (AES paper)](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~cc/vox/aesFinal.pdf); [Stanford MediaX summary](https://mediax.stanford.edu/research-projects/lat-chafe)
- (DOC/claim) Yamaha claims 4 ms for its YH-WL500 (non-Bluetooth link) against about 200 ms for ordinary Bluetooth headphones — [Yamaha YH-WL500](https://usa.yamaha.com/products/musical_instruments/guitars_basses/amps_accessories/yh-wl500/index.html). Ubisoft discourages Bluetooth for Rocksmith+ as too slow — [Ubisoft](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/game/rocksmith/plus/news-updates/0ObO0tPaR4kiGmmgpkeJZ/minimizing-audio-latency-on-console) (cited in the existing repo note). Retail and blog sources quote Bluetooth latency of roughly 34–250 ms by codec — [zZounds blog (OP)](https://blog.zzounds.com/?p=38894)
- (DOC, repo) Splitune's current default mapping (doc 12 §8):
  - Space/Enter = play/pause
  - ArrowLeft/PageUp = restart loop or previous bar
  - ArrowRight/PageDown = next bar
  - **ArrowDown/ArrowUp = slower/faster 5%**
  - C/N = clean/not yet; L = loop; M = mute

  It also has a "learn this pedal" remap and a PedalHud confirmation — `docs/specs/12-practice-screen-and-notation.md` §8

### Inferences
- **Conflict to fix:** a two-pedal AirTurn straight out of the box (Mode 2, Up/Down) would, under doc 12's defaults, **change tempo** instead of playing, pausing or restarting. The iRig BlueTurn in its Up/Down mode would do the same. Because a pedal and a keyboard are indistinguishable on the web (both send key events), first-run pedal setup ("press your left pedal… now your right") should be the default path whenever the user says they have a pedal. Alternatively, Up/Down should not default to tempo in practice mode.
- Musicians coming from forScore and OnSong expect one remap panel that covers keys, pedals and (where available) MIDI, with the current bindings discoverable on screen.
- Two-pedal devices give two inputs. The most valuable pair for practice is probably play/pause plus restart loop (repeat until stable), with tempo on a long-press or a third/fourth pedal. This is an inference to test.
- MIDI foot controllers and MIDI sustain pedals (AirTurn Mode 7) can only reach Splitune in Chromium and Firefox. On iPad web, keyboard-mode pedals are the only hands-free route, so the keyboard path must be complete on its own.
- The audio path the learner hears while playing along (stems plus click) should keep added output latency at or below about 25 ms, or the app should compensate visual and click timing. A Bluetooth headset adds roughly 150–200 ms and will make play-along feel late.

### Gaps
- I have not verified the actual keydown codes per mode for current AirTurn, PageFlip (Firefly/Cicada) and BlueTurn firmware on iPadOS Safari, Android Chrome and desktop. The repo note already lists this as an open experiment. I found no PageFlip key table.
- I found no published latency expectation for pedal-to-action response. The 0.1 s figure is a general HCI rule.
- I found no study of voice commands while an instrument plays.
- I could not confirm whether `AudioContext.outputLatency` reliably exposes Bluetooth latency on Tier 1 browsers.

## 4. Physical setup: distance, legibility, glanceability, orientation, wake lock, low light, headphones

### Takeaway
Players read from about 50–100 cm (stand) and further for some instruments, so notation must be larger than default print, and practice mode needs a distance-aware size setting. Screen Wake Lock is available in all Tier 1 engines but has had an iOS home-screen gap. Bluetooth audio is too laggy for play-along. Safe-listening guidance (WHO-ITU H.870) is relevant to long headphone sessions. I found no research on orientation or dark mode for music reading.

### Cited Findings
- (OP/guideline via forum) The Major Orchestra Librarians' Association treats an 8.5 mm staff (about 24 pt) as the most readable for instrumental parts, and parts may sit about 3 ft from the eye — [Musica Sacra forum](https://forum.musicasacra.com/forum/discussion/comment/179594)
- (DOC) Dorico calls 7 mm the most common staff size for parts. Larger staves are popular with players whose instruments keep them further from the stand (for example trombone, contrabass); handheld vocal music can be smaller — [Steinberg Dorico: changing the staff size](https://steinberg.help/dorico_first_steps/v5/en/dorico_first_steps/topics/first_steps_layout_formatting/first_steps_staff_size_changing_t.html)
- (OP) Forum players describe stand reading distance of about 21 in (about 525 mm) against about 14 in for a book, with some getting glasses prescribed for stand distance — [Piano World forum](https://forums.pianoworld.com//ubbthreads.php/topics/939522/1.html); [Violinist.com discussion](https://www.violinist.com/discussion/archive/7754)
- (EXP, abstract) A digital-music-stand study found some reduction in displayed music size was possible without significantly harming reading accuracy — [Bell et al. 2005, Waikato](https://researchcommons.waikato.ac.nz/entities/publication/921242c4-41c4-4cbc-a130-989da1517138) (cited in the existing repo note)
- (DOC) Screen Wake Lock has been supported since Safari 16.4 (2023-03-27), including iOS, and in Chromium and Firefox. MDN-derived data notes that **it did not work in standalone Home Screen web apps** (WebKit bug 254545). Later reports on how this was resolved conflict — [caniuse: Screen Wake Lock](https://caniuse.com/wake-lock); [web-features explorer](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/screen-wake-lock); [WebKit bug 254545](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=254545)
- (standard) WHO-ITU H.870 sets a weekly reference dose for adults equivalent to 80 dBA for 40 hours, and 75 dBA for 40 hours for children and sensitive users. Devices should offer a sound-allowance tracker, listening profiles and optional volume limiting — [WHO: Safe listening devices and systems](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241515276)
- (DOC) Bluetooth headphone latency: see §3 (about 200 ms typical, per Yamaha and Ubisoft).
- (DOC, repo) Doc 12 already makes "tablet on a stand" the primary layout, requires primary controls of at least 64 CSS px with the transport at the bottom edge on touch, and enables Screen Wake Lock in practice mode — `docs/specs/12-practice-screen-and-notation.md` §1, §2. The existing hands-busy note proposes Close 0.5 m / Stand 1 m / Far 2 m presets, and by arithmetic about 19 mm staff height at 2 m for the same visual angle as 8.5 mm at 0.9 m — `docs/research/ux/hands-busy-and-device-context.md`

### Inferences
- Use visual angle, not CSS px, to specify sizes. 8.5 mm at about 0.9 m subtends roughly 0.54°. At 60 cm (a laptop on a desk) that is about 5.7 mm; at 2 m (a phone on the floor, or a drummer's view) about 19 mm. Tab digits and chord names should be at least the staff-space-equivalent size for the chosen preset.
- Glanceability means one fixed "now" position plus a current-bar highlight that does not rely on colour (see §5), with the next bar visible so the eye can read ahead.
- Wake lock must be re-acquired on `visibilitychange`. Practice mode should show a non-blocking notice when the lock is unavailable (for example in an installed iOS PWA, if the bug persists).
- Practice sessions can run for hours on headphones. "Your part loud" (+6 dB) should never push the master output above unity without limiting; a session-time loudness reminder would follow the H.870 spirit (low priority).

### Gaps
- I found no study on landscape vs portrait or on dark mode and low-light reading of notation for musicians.
- I could not verify the MOLA 8.5 mm guideline at source (forum citation only), and found no controlled legibility-vs-distance study for screens.
- I found no data on typical device-to-player distances for guitar, bass, piano and drums in home practice.

## 5. Accessibility specific to music practice UIs

### Takeaway
WCAG 2.2 AA bites directly on practice UIs: target size (2.5.8), focus not obscured by sticky transports (2.4.11), dragging alternatives for loop handles and faders (2.5.7), pausable auto-scroll (2.2.2), colour-independent confidence (1.4.1, 1.4.11), single-key shortcuts that can be turned off or remapped (2.1.4), and three-flash limits for visual metronomes (2.3.1). Screen-reader access to tab and notation is an open research area: the working approaches are text or "talking" renderings per bar and braille output. Soundslice's accessibility is undocumented.

### Cited Findings
- (standard) 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum), AA: targets at least 24×24 CSS px, with spacing, equivalent-control, inline, user-agent and essential exceptions. 2.5.5 Target Size (Enhanced), AAA: 44×44 — [Understanding 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html); [Understanding 2.5.5](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-enhanced.html)
- (standard) 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum), AA: a component receiving keyboard focus must not be entirely hidden by author-created content, such as sticky transport bars or bottom sheets — [Understanding 2.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html)
- (standard) 2.5.7 Dragging Movements, AA: anything done by dragging must be achievable with a single pointer without dragging, unless dragging is essential — [Understanding 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)
- (standard) 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide, A: moving or scrolling content that starts automatically, lasts over 5 s and appears alongside other content needs a way to pause, stop or hide it — [Understanding 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)
- (standard) 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions, AAA: motion triggered by interaction can be disabled unless essential — [Understanding 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)
- (standard) 2.3.1 Three Flashes or Below Threshold, A: no more than three flashes in any one-second period unless below the general and red flash thresholds — [Understanding 2.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)
- (standard) 1.4.1 Use of Colour, A, and 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast, AA (3:1 for UI components and graphical objects) — [Understanding 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html); [Understanding 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)
- (standard) 2.1.4 Character Key Shortcuts, A: single-character shortcuts must be possible to turn off, remap, or limit to when a component has focus — [Understanding 2.1.4](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/character-key-shortcuts.html)
- (I did not re-open the WCAG Understanding pages in this session; the success-criterion wording above is from the normative WCAG 2.2 text.)
- (QUAL/research) Blind music learners face graphics-only notation software that outputs only visual print scores. Researchers built SoundCells, a browser-based text-notation tool with audio, print and braille output — [Learning Music Blind (Carleton)](https://cil.csit.carleton.ca/b/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Learning-Music-Blind-Understanding-Accessibility-Challenges-and-Future-Opportunities.pdf); [SoundCells (ACM, via Unpaywall)](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F3493612.3520462)
- (prototype) A student project noted that no "Talking Scores" existed for guitar tab and built a MusicXML-to-screen-reader-text converter for tab — [UW CSE 493E: Talking Guitar Tabs](https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse493e/24au/projects/guitar/index.html)
- (research) Lunar Tabs made online guitar tabs accessible to low-vision users, including spoken repetition information — [CSUN ScholarWorks record](https://scholarworks.csun.edu/handle/10211.3/133376) (record only)
- (DOC) The OpenSheetMusicDisplay braille plugin lets users read MusicXML scores with NVDA and JAWS — [OSMD Braille User Guide (netidee, 2026)](https://www.netidee.at/sites/default/files/2026-07/OSMD_Braille_User_Guide.pdf)
- (OP) A forum user describes MuseScore as having perhaps the best accessibility of the major notation programs — [MuseScore forum](https://musescore.org/en/node/323036)
- (DOC, repo) Splitune already specifies the Okabe-Ito palette, confidence that is never shown by colour alone (dashed border plus a "?" glyph for low confidence) and touch-target tiers — `docs/specs/12-practice-screen-and-notation.md` §4, §8; `docs/research/ux/accessibility.md`

### Inferences
- Doc 12's single-letter shortcuts (C, N, L, M) fall under 2.1.4. The existing "learn this pedal" remap may satisfy "remap", but a "turn off single-key shortcuts" setting is also needed.
- A visual metronome or beat flash (useful for deaf and hard-of-hearing users, and in loud rooms) can break 2.3.1 at tempos of 180 BPM and above (3 beats per second) if it flashes a large area. Keep the pulse small or low-contrast, or animate it as motion rather than a flash.
- Auto-scrolling notation meets 2.2.2 if pausing playback pauses the scroll. Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, switch from smooth scroll to page or line jumps; this is aligned with 2.3.3 even though that criterion is AAA.
- Loop handles, mixer faders and the tempo slider all need non-drag alternatives (2.5.7): tap bar A then bar B, steppers, or numeric fields. Doc 12 §5 already has tap-to-loop and numeric fields; the faders and tempo need the same.
- For screen-reader users the useful unit is the bar: a text rendering per bar on demand (chord, strings and frets, durations), not a live stream of every note during playback, which would be unusable.

### Gaps
- I found no documentation of Soundslice's screen-reader support.
- I found no evaluation of screen-reader users practising with loops and slow-down.
- Colour-vision-deficiency prevalence ("1 in 12 men, 1 in 200 women") is widely repeated, but I found only low-quality sources in this session; cite a primary epidemiology source before using a figure.
- I found no hearing-specific research on practice apps beyond the WHO safe-listening standard.

## 6. Instrument-specific needs (guitar/bass, piano, drums)

### Takeaway
Instrument research I found is thin. The concrete, defensible points are these. Guitar and bass need tab tied to tuning and capo and a mirrored left-handed fretboard (already specified). Piano needs hands-separate practice and a display that limits split attention; MIDI sustain pedals collide with "use a pedal for app control". Drummers have both feet busy and sit further away, so foot pedals and small text fail them.

### Cited Findings
- (EXP, n = 56 and 18) In piano learning, a piano-roll projected onto the keys beat Synthesia-style falling notes on a separate screen and beat sheet music on correct notes and cognitive load. This was a split-attention effect, tested on right hand only — [Rogers et al., P.I.A.N.O., ITS 2014](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/iui.inst.100/1-hci/hci-paper/2014/2014-gugenheimer-piano.pdf) (cited in the existing repo note)
- (DOC) AirTurn Mode 7 sends MIDI sustain-pedal messages (damper, sostenuto, soft), so a pianist's foot controller may be serving the instrument rather than the app — [AirTurn BT200S manual](https://manualslib.com/manual/1986862/Airturn-Bt200s-Series.html?page=13)
- (DOC) Players whose instruments keep them further from the stand prefer larger staves — [Steinberg Dorico](https://steinberg.help/dorico_first_steps/v5/en/dorico_first_steps/topics/first_steps_layout_formatting/first_steps_staff_size_changing_t.html)
- (DOC, repo) Left-handed users get mirrored chord diagrams and fretboard (not tab text) when `users.handedness='left'`. Doc 12 also notes that for drums "Minus one" removes the drum stem and the count-in click is the player's — `docs/specs/12-practice-screen-and-notation.md` §3, §7
- (DOC) Web MIDI (needed for e-kit pads or MIDI pedals as controls) is absent in Safari — [caniuse: Web MIDI](https://caniuse.com/midi)

### Inferences
- **Drums:** kick and hi-hat occupy both feet, so foot-pedal app control mostly fails. Drummers need loop auto-repeat with a count-in, "wait N bars then continue" behaviour, large-distance presets, and (on Chromium or Firefox) an optional spare e-kit pad mapped through Web MIDI. Play-along defaults should lean on the click, which is "the player's", plus minus-drums.
- **Piano:** hands-separate practice needs L/R hand separation in the transcription and a per-hand mute (left hand's part plays from the stem while you play the right). If the user's sustain pedal is MIDI, practice-mode bindings must ignore CC64 unless explicitly mapped.
- **Guitar/bass:** display tuning and capo up front, with tab frets relative to the capo. Chord shapes must respect the capo; left-handed users get mirrored diagrams only.

### Gaps
- I found no research on drum notation vs grid preferences, guitar capo or alternate-tuning UX, or bass-specific practice; these need user research or competitor analysis (Songsterr, Ultimate Guitar, Moises, Soundslice).
- I found no study of hands-separate practice efficacy for piano.

## 7. Objective: concrete, testable UX requirements derived from the evidence

### Takeaway
The practice screen must make three things effortless:
- jumping to and looping an exact bar
- changing tempo while repeating until it is stable
- controlling all of it without the hands leaving the instrument

It should default to bounded loops, a modest tempo ladder, a rest bar and an auto-generated log. It should measure retention (next-day first clean pass), not minutes or in-session scores. Where evidence is weak, the requirement says "default + A/B test".

### Gaps
- Numeric defaults below (70%, +5%, 3 clean passes, 1 rest bar, 25 ms, 100 ms) are starting points chosen from the cited evidence and competitor precedent, not evidence-derived constants. Each is listed with what to measure.

### Cited Findings: numbered UX requirements

**Practice loop, tempo and error correction**
1. **Loop the current bar in at most 2 inputs.** From a playing state, one tap, key or pedal sets a loop around the current phrase (default 2–4 bars, bar-snapped) and a second adjusts it. Test: median time-to-loop under 3 s in moderated tests; 0 drag gestures required. Basis: [Miksza (repeat section, whole-part-whole, skipping to the passage)](https://www.sciencegate.app/document/10.1177/0022429408317513); [Duke et al. 2009](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022429408328851).
2. **"Mark trouble spot" is a single pedal/key action during playback.** It drops a bar marker at the current position, and at the end of the pass the app offers "loop bars N–M". Measure: share of loops created from markers; next-day clean rate of marked bars. Basis: [Duke et al. 2009 (pinpoint the error location)](https://www.scribd.com/document/494219936/DukeEtAl2).
3. **Repeat until stable.** The pass counter shows consecutive clean passes (self-judged "felt clean / not yet", pedal-mappable). Default target: 3 consecutive clean passes before suggesting a tempo step. Basis: [Duke et al. 2009 (repeat until the error is absent in later trials)](https://www.scribd.com/document/494219936/DukeEtAl2).
4. **Tempo ladder as an editable default, not a rule.** Start at the last best clean tempo, or 70% if none. Step +5% after 3 clean passes and −5% after 2 failed ones. Allow a "test at full tempo" pass and optional 105–110% overspeed. Show both % and BPM. A/B the step size and pass count against next-day tempo. Basis: [Henley 2001 (tempo pattern null)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2307/3345799); [Soundslice speed training](https://www.soundslice.com/help/en/player/basic/286/speed-training/); [Bulletproof Musician on the limits of slow-only practice](https://bulletproofmusician.com/jason-sulliman-on-why-fast-practice-can-be-more-efficient-and-effective-than-slow-practice/).
5. **Experimental "ease into the trouble spot".** An optional mode that lowers tempo only in the bar before a marked trouble spot, modelled on expert behaviour. Label it experimental and measure clean-pass rate vs whole-loop slowing. Basis: [Duke et al. 2009](https://www.scribd.com/document/494219936/DukeEtAl2).
6. **Rest/think gap between repetitions.** Default 1 bar of rest plus a 1-bar count-in on loop restart (doc 12 options: none / 1 beat / 1 bar). Measure clean-pass rate with vs without the rest. Basis: [Palese & Duke 2022](https://cml.music.utexas.edu/sites/default/files/documents/paleseduke2022.pdf).
7. **Mixed practice is opt-in.** A "rotate my saved loops" mode cycles 2–3 loops in about 3-minute blocks. Blocked is the default for beginners. Show a one-line explanation that it feels harder but may stick better. Measure next-day first-pass results by condition. Basis: [Carter & Grahn 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4989027/); Stambaugh studies summarised there.
8. **Next-day warm-up.** On the first session after a sleep, offer "replay yesterday's hard bars at your last clean tempo" before new material. Basis: [Simmons 2011](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/distributed-practice-and-procedural-memory-consolidation-in-musicians-skill-learning/813362932149649409-5172); [Simmons & Duke 2006](https://www.jstor.org/stable/4151346).
9. **The primary progress metric is retention.** Use the tempo of the first clean pass of a loop in a new session, alongside `best_clean_tempo_pct`, rather than end-of-session tempo or minutes. Basis: [Carter & Grahn 2016 (blocked work declined Day 1 to Day 2)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4989027/); [Macnamara et al. 2014 (time is a weak proxy)](https://stafforini.com/works/macnamara-2014-deliberate-practice-and/).
10. **Record-and-compare in one action.** Record a take of the current loop and play it back A/B against the isolated reference stem, with a neutral prompt ("What differed?") and an option to listen later. Basis: [Palese & Duke 2022](https://cml.music.utexas.edu/sites/default/files/documents/paleseduke2022.pdf); [Frontiers 2025 self-listening](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1568021/full); [Bulletproof Musician on Silveira & Gavin](https://bulletproofmusician.com/evidence-that-youre-probably-not-great-at-performing-and-critiquing-your-performance-at-the-same-time/).
11. **"Listen first" pass.** One action plays the loop once with only the user's isolated part, as a cue to imagine or sing along, then starts the playing pass with a count-in. Basis: [Steenstrup et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.757052/full).
12. **Mix progression.** Use "Your part loud" for accuracy work and suggest "Original" or "Minus one" once the loop is clean at 90% or more of the original tempo. Measure the share of passes in minus-one per song. Basis: [Juntunen et al. via Music Teacher](https://musicteachermagazine.co.uk/content/feature/backing-tracks-good-or-bad).
13. **Groove first, click optional.** With stems available the band is the timing reference and the click is off by default. The click follows the detected beat grid, and an optional "gap click" mutes it for N bars to test the internal pulse. Basis: [Owens & Cunningham](https://wrexham.repository.guildhe.ac.uk/id/eprint/17318/1/More%20Cowbell_Variations_inc%20c%20sheet.pdf); [Hanberry 2004 (metronome no advantage)](https://repository.lsu.edu/gradschool_dissertations/1187/).

**Motivation and habit**
14. **Resume in one action.** The home or practice entry offers "Continue: <song>, bars N–M at X%". Measure: median time from app open to first audible pass, target under 10 s. Basis: [Fender abandonment claim](https://www.guitarworld.com/news/90-percent-of-new-guitarists-abandon-the-instrument-within-a-year-according-to-fender); [adult learners define success by enjoyment](https://ayo.com.au/why-am-i-doing-this-an-insight-into-the-experiences-of-adult-learners-of-musical-instruments/).
15. **Proximal, data-generated session goal.** Propose one concrete goal ("bars 17–20 clean at 80%") that the user can edit. Never use a minutes target as the default. Basis: [MTNA review on expert goals](https://certification.mtna.org/downloads/Informed/EJ/AOY/2024_E-Journal_AOY.pdf); [IU practice-journal thesis](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/dspace/items/dce1cb2f-648e-4a16-821a-766175e818cf).
16. **Automatic practice log.** Log loops, tempos and clean/not-yet judgements without manual entry. Reflection is one optional tap. Basis: [Holmes-Davis 2015](https://open.bu.edu/items/6a4e02ef-3c43-4cdc-b1a3-d446f5491900); [Miksza 2013 SRL training (secondary)](https://blog.sonicbids.com/research-tested-practice-strategies-that-will-help-you-learn-new-pieces-faster).
17. **Informational feedback, forgiving consistency.** Progress copy reports competence ("clean at 80%, up from 70%"). Any streak is weekly, with grace days and no loss animation. Measure: reminder opt-out rate and D7/D30 return. Basis: [Evans 2015 (SDT)](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/2015_Evans_SDT_MusicEdu.pdf); [Duolingo streak frustration](https://eprints.umpo.ac.id/21371/); [Hamari et al. 2014](https://www.lescahiersdelinnovation.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/2014-hamari_et_al-does_gamification_work.pdf).

**Hands-busy input**
18. **First-run pedal setup.** When a user indicates they have a pedal, a 2-step "press left / press right" flow binds whatever keys the device sends. Defaults: left pedal = restart loop, right pedal = play/pause. Test: in each of AirTurn modes 2/3/5 and the three iRig BlueTurn modes, a working pair is bound in under 30 s on iPadOS Safari, Android Chrome and desktop Chrome. Basis: [AirTurn BT200S modes (default Mode 2 = Up/Down)](https://manualslib.com/manual/1986862/Airturn-Bt200s-Series.html?page=13); [iRig BlueTurn modes](https://www.musicjunction.com.au/products/irig-blueturn-bluetooth-page-turner).
19. **Fix the Up/Down default conflict.** In practice mode an unconfigured ArrowUp/ArrowDown must not silently change tempo, because it is the AirTurn factory default output. Either show the PedalHud with "Pedal detected? Set it up" on the first Up/Down press, or move the tempo defaults to other keys. Basis: doc 12 §8 vs the [AirTurn manual](https://manualslib.com/manual/1986862/Airturn-Bt200s-Series.html?page=13).
20. **One remap panel** for keys, pedals and (where supported) MIDI, which also lists the current bindings, like forScore's "Page Turners & Shortcuts". A "turn off single-key shortcuts" toggle is needed for WCAG 2.1.4. Basis: [forScore](https://forscore.co/?p=1641); [Understanding 2.1.4](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/character-key-shortcuts.html).
21. **Confirm every action within 100 ms.** Each key or pedal action shows the PedalHud (and optionally an audible tick) within 100 ms. Transport starts land on a beat via count-in. Test with automated timing in E2E. Basis: [NN/g response-time limits](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/).
22. **MIDI is feature-detected.** Offer MIDI pedal, pad and foot-controller binding only where `navigator.requestMIDIAccess` exists. On Safari, keyboard-mode pedals must cover every action. Ignore CC64 (sustain) unless explicitly bound. Basis: [caniuse Web MIDI](https://caniuse.com/midi); [WebKit bug 107250](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=107250).
23. **Voice and face gestures stay off the critical path.** At most an optional, push-to-talk closed vocabulary later, always with a pedal or key equivalent. Basis: [forScore face-gesture requirements](https://forscore.co/face-gestures/) and [mixed user reports](https://www.classicalguitardelcamp.com/viewtopic.php?p=1687299); [MDN SpeechRecognition](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/SpeechRecognition).
24. **Pedal keys never scroll or click.** In practice mode pedal and transport keys never scroll the page or activate the focused control (for example, Space on a focused button). The sticky transport must never fully cover the focused element. Test: keyboard-only E2E with sticky transport open. Basis: [Understanding 2.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html); doc 12 §8 focus sink.

**Physical setup and audio**
25. **Distance presets by visual angle.** Close / Stand / Far, with Stand as the tablet default. Each preset renders staff, tab digits and chord names at the visual angle of 8.5 mm at about 0.9 m (≈0.54°). Far shows 1–2 bars. Test: legibility at 0.6 m, 1 m and 2 m with real users and devices. Basis: [MOLA via forum](https://forum.musicasacra.com/forum/discussion/comment/179594); [Dorico staff sizes](https://steinberg.help/dorico_first_steps/v5/en/dorico_first_steps/topics/first_steps_layout_formatting/first_steps_staff_size_changing_t.html).
26. **Glanceable "now".** A fixed now-position, a current-bar outline that does not depend on colour, and the next bar always visible.
27. **Wake lock is held for the whole practice session.** Request Screen Wake Lock on entering practice mode and re-acquire it on `visibilitychange`. Show a non-blocking notice if it is unavailable. Test: no screen dimming over 15 minutes on iPadOS Safari (tab and installed PWA), Android Chrome and desktop. Basis: [caniuse wake-lock](https://caniuse.com/wake-lock); [WebKit bug 254545](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=254545).
28. **Latency-aware play-along.** Read `outputLatency` where it is exposed and offer a one-tap latency calibration (tap along with the click) that offsets the cursor and click. Warn when wireless output is likely ("wireless headphones can lag about 200 ms; use wired for playing along"). Target: added monitoring offset at or below 25 ms after calibration. Basis: [Chafe & Gurevich 2004](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~cc/vox/aesFinal.pdf); [Yamaha latency claim](https://usa.yamaha.com/products/musical_instruments/guitars_basses/amps_accessories/yh-wl500/index.html).
29. **Safe loudness.** Mixer presets never push master output above unity without a limiter. Long-session listening guidance is optional. Basis: [WHO-ITU H.870](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241515276).

**Accessibility**
30. **Non-colour encoding.** Confidence, loop state, clean/not-yet and rough-stem warnings each use shape, pattern or text plus colour, at 3:1 non-text contrast. Test with CVD simulation in Storybook. Basis: [Understanding 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html); [Understanding 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html).
31. **Reduced motion.** Auto-scroll pauses with playback. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` notation advances by page or line jumps instead of smooth scroll. Basis: [Understanding 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html); [Understanding 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html).
32. **Non-drag alternatives.** Loop handles, faders and the tempo slider each have tap or stepper alternatives. Basis: [Understanding 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html).
33. **Safe visual metronome.** An optional beat pulse for deaf and hard-of-hearing users or loud rooms, limited to a small area or low contrast so that it never breaks the three-flash rule at tempos of 180 BPM and above. Basis: [Understanding 2.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html).
34. **Screen-reader bar view.** An on-demand text rendering of the current or selected bar (chord; string and fret or pitch; duration), with no live announcements during playback unless requested. Basis: [Talking Guitar Tabs](https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse493e/24au/projects/guitar/index.html); [OSMD braille](https://www.netidee.at/sites/default/files/2026-07/OSMD_Braille_User_Guide.pdf); [Learning Music Blind](https://cil.csit.carleton.ca/b/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Learning-Music-Blind-Understanding-Accessibility-Challenges-and-Future-Opportunities.pdf).

**Instrument-specific**
35. **Drums: no foot-pedal dependence.** Auto-repeat with count-in, a "continue after N passes" option, the Far preset by default, and an optional e-kit pad binding via Web MIDI where available. Basis: inference from §6; [caniuse Web MIDI](https://caniuse.com/midi).
36. **Piano: hands-separate practice.** A per-hand mute (the other hand plays from the stem) and one primary representation at a time to limit split attention. Basis: [Rogers et al. 2014](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/iui.inst.100/1-hci/hci-paper/2014/2014-gugenheimer-piano.pdf).
37. **Guitar/bass: tuning and capo up front.** Tab frets are relative to the capo, and left-handed users get mirrored diagrams only (doc 12 §3). Validate with user research, because no research source was found.
